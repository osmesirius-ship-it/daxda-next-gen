Why does Bz = -2.85 nT matter to a human on Earth?
Earth’s natural magnetic field points North. When the incoming solar wind's magnetic vector (Bz) points South (negative), the two opposing field lines reconnect like snapping magnets. This unlocks Earth's dayside shield, allowing charged solar particles to flood into the upper atmosphere, sparking auroras and satellite drag.
What does the USGS Astrogeology Feed show?
This is a direct feed from the USGS Astrogeology PlanetaryMaps WMS server, displaying the global MDIM21 Viking mosaic and MOLA elevation baseline. It allows mapping crater morphology, volcanic tholi, yardangs, and paleomagnetic crustal remanence across the Martian surface.
What are MAST & NExScI tracking?
MAST (STScI) tracks deep-space targets observed by the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) and Hubble, while Caltech NExScI provides confirmed exoplanets detected by TESS and Kepler, reporting their radius, orbital period, and distance from Earth.
