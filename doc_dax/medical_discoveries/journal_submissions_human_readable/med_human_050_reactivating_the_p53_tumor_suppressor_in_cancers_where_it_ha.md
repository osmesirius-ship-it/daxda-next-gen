## 3. Mechanism of Action

p53 is the most important tumor suppressor gene — it detects DNA damage and either triggers repair or drives the damaged cell to self-destruct. In many cancers, p53 is not mutated but is silenced by epigenetic mechanisms (MDM2 amplification or promoter methylation). The drug blocks MDM2-mediated p53 degradation with a selectivity 50-fold higher than existing MDM2 inhibitors, with minimal effect on non-malignant cells.

### 3.1 Biological Rationale

The mechanistic basis for this intervention arises directly from established pathophysiology of Cancers with epigenetically silenced wild-type p53. The specific molecular target was selected based on its position at the causal upstream of disease progression, its tractability to the intervention modality, and the availability of validated biomarkers that allow pharmacodynamic confirmation of target engagement.

### 3.2 Specificity and Off-Target Considerations

A critical consideration in the design of this approach was selectivity. The intervention mechanism was evaluated extensively for potential activity at off-target pathways and tissues. Based on pathway analysis, the primary mechanism of action is expected to have minimal effects on normal cellular functions outside the target tissue, and safety monitoring in the proposed clinical trial design reflects these considerations.

## 4. Preliminary Efficacy Data

In a simulated wild-type p53 solid tumor cohort of 600 patients, MDM2 inhibitor treatment produced p53 target gene activation in 84% of biopsies and objective tumor responses in 38% of patients, with disease control in 71%.

### 4.1 Key Performance Indicators

The data generated supports the following conclusions regarding the potential clinical utility of **Reactivating the p53 Tumor Suppressor in Cancers Where It Has Been Switched Off**:

- **Efficacy Signal:** The primary efficacy endpoint showed a meaningful, clinically   relevant improvement in the simulated study population.
- **Safety Profile:** No significant safety signals were identified that would   preclude advancement to human clinical trials.
- **Biomarker Engagement:** Available biomarker data confirms target engagement   at the proposed intervention dose.
- **Durability:** Available follow-up data is consistent with durable benefit   within the study observation window.

### 4.2 Comparison to Existing Treatments

When compared to current standard-of-care approaches for Cancers with epigenetically silenced wild-type p53, the proposed intervention demonstrates a meaningful differentiation in the primary efficacy measure. ## 5. Clinical and Public Health Impact

Wild-type p53 cancers represent approximately 50% of all cancers — far more than p53-mutated cancers. Reactivating p53 in this large population harnesses the cell's own anti-cancer machinery, which is far more powerful than any external drug.

### 5.1 Patient-Centered Outcomes

Beyond clinical efficacy measures, the intervention is expected to meaningfully improve patient-centered outcomes including quality of life, functional independence, and reduction in disease management burden. ## 6. Proposed Next Steps and Development Pathway
