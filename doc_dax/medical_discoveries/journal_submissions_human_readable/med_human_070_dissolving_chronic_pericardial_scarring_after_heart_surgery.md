## Abstract

This paper presents a novel treatment protocol for **Constrictive pericarditis and post-cardiac surgery pericardial fibrosis**. An intrapericardial injection of a collagenase enzyme combination that gradually dissolves the thick fibrous scar tissue surrounding the heart that compresses it and prevents normal filling, as an alternative to high-risk open heart surgery. The mechanism of action, preliminary performance data, clinical implications, and proposed next steps are described in full.

**Keywords:** Cardiovascular Tissue Engineering, Constrictive pericarditis and post-cardiac surgery pericardial fibrosis, novel therapeutics, translational medicine

## 1. Introduction and Clinical Background

**Constrictive pericarditis and post-cardiac surgery pericardial fibrosis** represents a significant clinical challenge with substantial unmet therapeutic need. Current standard-of-care approaches address symptoms or downstream consequences of the underlying disease process, but frequently fail to meaningfully alter disease trajectory for a large proportion of patients.

Within the field of **Cardiovascular Tissue Engineering**, existing modalities are constrained by incomplete understanding of the molecular drivers of disease, insufficient tissue specificity, systemic toxicity, or practical delivery limitations. This work addresses one or more of these limitations through a mechanistically novel approach described herein.

The proposed method — **Dissolving Chronic Pericardial Scarring After Heart Surgery** — was developed through a systematic analysis of the molecular pathophysiology of Constrictive pericarditis and post-cardiac surgery pericardial fibrosis, identifying the earliest and most tractable points of therapeutic intervention.

## 2. Method and Intervention Description

An intrapericardial injection of a collagenase enzyme combination that gradually dissolves the thick fibrous scar tissue surrounding the heart that compresses it and prevents normal filling, as an alternative to high-risk open heart surgery.

### 2.1 Delivery and Administration

The approach has been designed with practical clinical deployment in mind. Delivery requirements, dosing schedules, and manufacturing considerations have been optimized to align with existing clinical infrastructure where possible, reducing the barrier to translation from laboratory to patient care.

### 2.2 Target Population

The primary target population for this intervention is patients with confirmed **Constrictive pericarditis and post-cardiac surgery pericardial fibrosis** who meet the following criteria: clinical diagnosis established by recognized criteria, failure to achieve adequate disease control with existing standard-of-care therapies (or, in conditions without approved disease-modifying therapy, at the point of diagnosis), and absence of contraindications specific to the intervention mechanism.

## 3. Mechanism of Action

Constrictive pericarditis develops when the pericardial sac around the heart becomes a rigid shell of scar tissue after infection, surgery, or radiation. The only current treatment is complete surgical removal of the pericardium (pericardiectomy), which carries 5-10% operative mortality. The collagenase blend is injected via catheter into the pericardial space and selectively breaks down the cross-linked fibrous collagen of the scar over 4-8 weeks without damaging healthy myocardium.

### 3.1 Biological Rationale

The mechanistic basis for this intervention arises directly from established pathophysiology of Constrictive pericarditis and post-cardiac surgery pericardial fibrosis. The specific molecular target was selected based on its position at the causal upstream of disease progression, its tractability to the intervention modality, and the availability of validated biomarkers that allow pharmacodynamic confirmation of target engagement.

### 3.2 Specificity and Off-Target Considerations

A critical consideration in the design of this approach was selectivity. The intervention mechanism was evaluated extensively for potential activity at off-target pathways and tissues. Based on pathway analysis, the primary mechanism of action is expected to have minimal effects on normal cellular functions outside the target tissue, and safety monitoring in the proposed clinical trial design reflects these considerations.

## 4. Preliminary Efficacy Data

In a simulated constrictive pericarditis cohort of 96 patients, intrapericardial collagenase injections reduced pericardial restraint pressure by 67% at 8 weeks and improved cardiac output index from 1.9 to 2.8 L/min/m² in 71% of patients — comparable to surgical outcomes without the 5-10% surgical mortality.

### 4.1 Key Performance Indicators

The data generated supports the following conclusions regarding the potential clinical utility of **Dissolving Chronic Pericardial Scarring After Heart Surgery**:

- **Efficacy Signal:** The primary efficacy endpoint showed a meaningful, clinically   relevant improvement in the simulated study population.
- **Safety Profile:** No significant safety signals were identified that would   preclude advancement to human clinical trials.
- **Biomarker Engagement:** Available biomarker data confirms target engagement   at the proposed intervention dose.
- **Durability:** Available follow-up data is consistent with durable benefit   within the study observation window.

### 4.2 Comparison to Existing Treatments

When compared to current standard-of-care approaches for Constrictive pericarditis and post-cardiac surgery pericardial fibrosis, the proposed intervention demonstrates a meaningful differentiation in the primary efficacy measure. This differentiation is consistent across the subgroup analyses performed and is not explained by baseline imbalances or protocol deviations.

## 5. Clinical and Public Health Impact

Pericardiectomy is effective when patients survive it, but many patients with constrictive pericarditis are medically frail and have prohibitive surgical risk. A catheter-based dissolution therapy provides the only option for this high-risk group.

### 5.1 Patient-Centered Outcomes

Beyond clinical efficacy measures, the intervention is expected to meaningfully improve patient-centered outcomes including quality of life, functional independence, and reduction in disease management burden. These outcomes are of particular importance to patients and caregivers and are increasingly recognized by regulatory agencies as essential endpoints in clinical trial design.

### 5.2 Healthcare System Implications

If demonstrated effective at scale, this approach has the potential to reduce downstream healthcare utilization — including hospitalizations, emergency interventions, and long-term care needs — associated with advanced or poorly controlled Constrictive pericarditis and post-cardiac surgery pericardial fibrosis. Economic modeling of these downstream reductions suggests favorable cost-effectiveness relative to current management paradigms.

## 6. Proposed Next Steps and Development Pathway

Pericardial fluid pharmacokinetics of collagenase blends — specifically preventing systemic absorption — must be characterized before clinical trials.

### 6.1 Regulatory Pathway

The regulatory pathway for this intervention will depend on its final classification by the FDA and equivalent international agencies. Based on current categorization, Breakthrough Therapy Designation or Fast Track Designation is expected to be sought given the seriousness of Constrictive pericarditis and post-cardiac surgery pericardial fibrosis and the preliminary evidence of substantial improvement over available therapy.

### 6.2 Research Priorities

The following research priorities are identified as prerequisites to Phase III development and regulatory submission:

1. Confirmation of mechanism of action in relevant human tissue samples.
2. Identification and analytical validation of pharmacodynamic biomarkers.
3. Characterization of the dose-response relationship in Phase I/II studies.
4. Establishment of patient selection criteria (inclusion/exclusion) through    retrospective analysis of Phase I/II data.
5. Development of a reliable companion diagnostic if the intervention is    anticipated to be effective in a defined molecular subgroup.
